# Method and evidence

The implementation follows the structure of Xu, Zhang, and Wong (2010), with experimental controls for the four conflicting or suspicious expressions in the printed method. The author-hosted PDF is the reference for the notation. Its equations and figures were inspected as rendered pages. The slides, example pages, animation, and user study are supplemental evidence. [Author's paper and supplements](https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/asciiart/asciiart.html) · [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1778789)

| Component | Paper or supplement | Implementation decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input, §3 and Fig. 4 | Polylines are the basic input. Raster line art is first vectorized. The paper depicts separate vertices on an extracted line drawing. | Accept native polyline JSON or PNG/JPG. For raster, composite alpha, apply EXIF orientation, infer polarity from median border luminance unless specified, threshold luminance at 128, thin using Zhang–Suen, trace and simplify by 1 source pixel. |
| Vector preparation, §3 | The paper uses polylines and moves their vertices. It does not specify tracing or vertex spacing. | Remove consecutive duplicate points, retain shared coordinates and explicit closures, and subdivide edges into equal lengths up to 24 matching pixels. Original short edges are kept. Solitary points remain dots. Raster tracing follows maximal degree-two chains and traces cycles separately. The diagonal rule prevents a shortcut where an orthogonal neighbor already connects the corner. Simplification preserves traced junction endpoints and keeps original cycles if simplification would reduce them below three distinct vertices. Native crossings without an explicit shared vertex keep separate paths. |
| Glyph preparation and rasterization, §3 | Extract glyph centerlines, vectorize as polylines, and re-rasterize glyphs and input strokes at one pixel width. Authors use fixed-width glyphs and experiment with 95 printable ASCII and 475 Shift-JIS characters. | Bundle Liberation Mono Regular 2.1.5 under OFL. Rasterize the font at 4×, threshold at 128, thin, trace, simplify by one supersample pixel, then scale down the vectors and draw with the same Bresenham implementation as input. Pixel indexing floors nonnegative coordinates and canonicalizes the endpoint order. |
| Font and cell placement | Exact optimizer font, glyph outlines, glyph pixel dimensions and baseline were not specified in the paper or slides. The author-hosted queen HTML requests Terminal for plain-text display. | Base size 20 px, nominal advance 12 px, cell 12 × 20 px, left origin at 0, baseline at y=15. At base size Pillow reports ascent 17 and descent 6. The chosen cell contains the printable glyph ink vertically. The horizontal overhang of `%` and `_` clips at the cell boundary. Both matching and rendering use the same clipping and proportional baseline at their respective raster scales. |
| AISS, §4, Fig. 6, Eq. 1 | Regular anchors, approximately every other pixel, with shared log-polar scale. Sum grayness in 5 radial and 12 angular bins, radius about half the shorter side, after a 7 × 7 Gaussian. Sum the per-anchor histogram norms and divide by both shapes' total ink. | Anchors are `(1, 1), (3, 1), …` in zero-indexed raster coordinates. Radial outer edges are log-spaced from 1 px through half the shorter side. Zero belongs to the innermost bin and the final edge is included. Angular bins start at +x and follow clockwise screen coordinates. Gaussian sigma is 1.4 with zero padding at each cell. Normalize by the unfiltered ink sum to keep border loss out of the denominator. Use L2 within each anchor and sum over anchors. |
| Matching, §4 and Fig. 7 | Best glyph over the complete character set. The paper does not detail zero mass or tie handling. | Empty tiles map to space at zero error. Space competes in nonempty tiles and those tiles still count in the occupied-cell normalization. Ties use glyph order. Cache matches by raster bytes. |
| Local deformation, §5, Eq. 2 | Maximum of exponential angle and length penalties with weights `8/pi`, `2/min(Tw,Th)`, and `0.5`. The rendered ratio term gives `exp(0.5)` at identity while the prose says identity costs 1. | Default subtracts 1 inside the ratio exponent. `relative_length="printed"` uses the literal ratio. A collapsed nontrivial segment or accessibility link is invalid. A true dot has unit local cost. |
| Accessibility, §5, Fig. 8b, Eqs. 3–4 | Cast rays from segment midpoints, obtain surrounding segments and their nearest points, and combine link deformation with local deformation by maximum. Printed weights are proportional to distance. Prose says closer segments receive more weight. | Use 32 uniform ray directions. Keep the first hit in each direction, deduplicate segments, and project onto each target segment. We omit self hits, collinear rays, dot targets and initially zero-length links. Store visibility, projection fractions and normalized original-distance weights. During annealing, evaluate the link between the moved source midpoint and the same fraction on the moved target. Use inverse distance by default and direct distance in `accessibility_weights="printed"`. Zero rays runs local-only. The paper gives no ray count, visibility update rule or rule for tracking projected points. |
| Cell deformation, §5, Eq. 5 | Length-weighted average of partial or whole segments in each cell. | Split geometry exactly at character-grid boundaries and weight by the lengths after the move. Half-open cell ownership handles boundary-aligned lines. An inked tile with zero continuous length, for example from a dot or raster endpoint, gets deformation 1. |
| Objective, §5, Eq. 6 | Average the products of matching error and deformation over occupied cells. | Use occupied raster tiles in the proposed state for `K`, including cells that match a space. Empty drawings have objective zero. The incremental path rebuilds the raster for geometrically affected cells and reevaluates deformation for linked segments. A full recomputation is available for verification. |
| Proposals, §5 | Select one vertex at random and displace it by at most the larger cell dimension. The displacement distribution is unspecified. | Default samples a uniform nonzero integer offset from a disk and counts out-of-canvas moves as rejected. The alternate `proposal="uniform_radius"` uses a uniform continuous direction and radius, favoring shorter moves per unit area. |
| Temperature and acceptance, §5 | The rendered schedule shows a positive power of the iteration. The printed comparison accepts uphill proposals when probability is below the uniform draw. Taken literally, it favors jumping across large barriers at a fixed temperature. | Default uses `0.2 * ta * c**(-0.997)` and standard Metropolis acceptance `u < exp(-delta/t)`. `ta` is mean initial AISS error across all cells including empty cells. Equal energies are accepted in Metropolis. The `"printed"` modes independently use the positive exponent and reversed comparison. The geometric alternative uses `0.2 * ta * 0.997**(c-1)`. These options are hypotheses about the intended behavior. |
| Termination, §5 and Fig. 10 | Terminate after 5,000 consecutive iterations without lower energy. The example illustrates an improving sequence. | Reset patience only on a new best energy (tolerance `1e-12`), count all proposed steps, retain the best state and record the last state separately. The source does not settle whether to stop on stagnation relative to current or historical best, or which state to return after an uphill move. |
| Resolution, §3 and Fig. 12 | The printed formula is `ceil(H / (alpha * ceil(W / Rw)))`. The paper suggests comparing optimized energies across widths. | Implement that formula, then uniformly scale and center in the full character grid with minimum two pixels of padding per side. Use a centered inferred viewbox for an axis-aligned stroke or dot without a canvas. Record energies across resolutions for comparison. The experiments also inspect visual continuity because Eq. 6 values alone do not score recognizability. |

## Reference material

- The author's Figure 12 queen input is available as a standalone raster from the supplement. The page also contains a side-by-side image and a plain-text result. The train and Golden Temple supplements provide separate raster inputs and published results. Their posted source is colored or filled, and the paper does not include the pre-optimization polylines. The church photo and edge diagram are available in the paper. The exact Figure 7 query pixels, optimizer source/config, glyph bank, and full reference polyline sets were not located. [Queen](https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/asciiart/queen/queen.htm) · [Train](https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/asciiart/train/train.htm) · [Golden Temple](https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/asciiart/goldentemple/goldentemple.htm)
- The slides corroborate the 5 × 12 descriptor and show comparisons and the two deformation constraints. The optimization supplement supplies a GIF with 98 frames. Neither identifies the proposed corrections or explains the relationship between those frames and vertex proposals. Fig. 10 displays iteration 155 while the text lists 5,000 iterations of patience. It is unclear whether the figure counts display checkpoints, accepted moves, a subset of a run, or another iteration unit. [Slides](https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/asciiart/asciiart.pps) · [Optimization GIF](https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/asciiart/optimization/optimization.gif)
- Coumar and Kingston's independent study is useful context for tile-matching limitations. Its released AISS baseline uses one anchor per tile, mean absolute bin differences and Arial rasters. It does not verify multi-anchor Eq. 1 or the constrained optimizer. [Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14375) · [Baseline implementation](https://github.com/saiccoumar/deep_ascii_converter/blob/main/convert_static.py)
